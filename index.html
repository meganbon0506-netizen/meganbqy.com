I embrace chaos, diving into personal investigations and carving out structure where there is none.

During my time at P&G, I worked on a project with a broad question:

"How do we increase sales of our premium beauty device?"

That was it — no further instructions, no further details. So... I got crackin'.

A market problem that looked like pricing, turned out to be something

much deeper.
01
Understanding the market

Category Landscape

Beauty devices = high consideration purchase.

Purchase decision drivers:

✓  safety

✓  efficacy

✓  pain level

✓  price

Sources: Review sites, forums, product listing pages

Understanding the market leader

• Premium positioning

• Seen as effective and safe

• Credibility built through review volume

Understanding our own product:

• Premium positioning

• ~30% price premium vs. market leader

• Key differentiation: Additional safety features

Safety mattered. So why weren't we winning?

02
Forming the hypothesis

Research + gut instinct = hypothesis

Consumers perceived competitors as "safe enough."
So added safety didn't justify the premium.

03
Testing hypothesis and validating at scale

20 in-depth interviews
across target segments.

Conducted quantitative survey with 200 respondents

The Real Answer

This wasn't a pricing problem.

It was an education problem.

When consumers understood why our safety feature mattered, they chose us.

Exhibit B

I noticed the same pattern when I decided to write a children's book for the first time.

I knew from the get-go that I didn't just want to make a book that's good for an amateur. I wanted it to hold its own even when placed among others on a shelf.

I found myself asking:

"How do I make something people would actually want to buy and keep?"

"How do I give this longevity and build something bigger than a single release?"

So I went from 0 to 100, and worked on it like a professional.

market & readers

Identifying the target audience: While we need to appeal to children 4–6, parents are the actual purchasers — so we need to target both.

Finding our place in the market: Values like 'kindness' and 'respect' are over-saturated. Target newer-age values like vulnerability and independence — relevant to younger millennial parents.

story & writing

Referencing popular books to guide language complexity and story length:

Short sentences. Repetition builds rhythm. One core emotion.

visual direction

Iterative process to both appeal to readers yet stand out.

Rhino on two legs — more relatable.

Too cookie-cutter. Can we do something different?

Cracked it!

product & design

Obsessing over...

• Paper • Binding • Size • Finish • Price

Market research.

Working with suppliers on samples.

THE RESULT?

I sold out my first run of 100 books.

Through word-of-mouth alone. No ads, no paid promotion — just a product people genuinely wanted to share.
